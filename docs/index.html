    How it works

    Four steps, each tested on data it never trained on. Full code and results are on GitHub.

    1. Read the rivers

      Daily flow at six Brahmaputra stations from Dibrugarh to Dhubri, plus the Subansiri, Jia Bharali, Dhansiri, Kopili, Beki and Barak (GloFAS, 1997–2026).

    2. Learn how surges travel

      A gradient-boosted model per lead time uses each station's recent rise and every upstream station's, and predicts whether flow reaches the warning level within 1–7 days.

    3. Predict impact, not just water

      River forecasts plus each district's flood history from other years give the chance that ASDMA reports 1,000+ people affected that month.

    4. See the water from space

      A U-Net maps floodwater on Sentinel-1 radar, so forecasts can be checked against where water actually spread.

    Built to avoid fooling itself

    • Flood levels and alert cut-offs come from training years only.
    • A day's inputs are identical whether or not later days exist; automated tests check this on every change.
    • Each test year is forecast with a model that has never seen it.
    • Live forecasts are logged before events and scored afterwards.

    Limitations

    • River flow is from the GloFAS reanalysis, a model, not gauge readings.
    • ASDMA's district records start in 2021 and are monthly.
    • No rainfall yet. It is the next addition and should help most with local floods.
    • A research project, not an official warning service.

    Data: river discharge from GloFAS v4 via Open-Meteo · flood reports from ASDMA's daily FRIMS bulletins, compiled by CivicDataLab's IDS-DRR project · radar scenes and labels from Sen1Floods11 (Bonafilia et al., 2020) · district boundaries from Census 2011 shapefiles.
